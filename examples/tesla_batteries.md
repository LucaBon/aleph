# Lithium-ion battery longevity in electric vehicles

Tesla Model S vehicles use lithium-ion battery packs. Under normal driving
conditions and with Tesla's thermal management system, these packs are expected
to retain about 90% of their original capacity after 200,000 miles, according
to Tesla's 2024 impact report. Independent teardown analyses of Model S
vehicles with over 300,000 miles have generally corroborated that figure,
finding average capacity retention of 87-92% in high-mileage fleets.

Degradation is non-linear. Most capacity loss happens in the first 25,000
miles (typically 3-5%), after which the curve flattens substantially.
Frequent DC fast charging, extreme ambient temperatures, and prolonged
storage at 100% state of charge all accelerate degradation.

## Chemistry variations

Standard Range Model 3 and Model Y vehicles ship with lithium iron phosphate
(LFP) cells, which tolerate more charge cycles than the nickel-cobalt-aluminum
(NCA) cells used in Long Range trims. LFP cells handle about 3,000 full
charge cycles to 80% capacity, versus roughly 1,500 for NCA. However, NCA
cells have higher energy density — about 260 Wh/kg compared to LFP's 160 Wh/kg.

## Replacement costs

As of 2025, Tesla quotes battery pack replacement at $12,000 to $18,000
depending on the vehicle and battery version. Third-party refurbishment
typically runs $8,000 to $14,000. Neither cost includes labor.

An older report from a Tesla service center stated Tesla batteries last 8
years on average before requiring replacement. This figure is frequently
cited online but has been superseded by warranty data and fleet studies
showing most packs last well beyond 10 years under typical use.
